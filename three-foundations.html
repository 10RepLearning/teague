The center

The Learner

What serves this student?

Each foundation answers a different question, and all three point to the same place. Tyler asks why, Schwab asks who, and Tomlinson asks how. When the learner sits at the center, the politics of curriculum become a shared judgment about students rather than a contest over who prevails.

  • Why? Purpose
  • Who? Balance
  • How? Response

In practice

A curriculum decision can be tested against all three at once: Does it serve a clear purpose? Were the right voices at the table? Can teachers adapt it for the students in front of them?

Ralph Tyler · Purpose

Why?

What educational purposes should the school seek to attain?

Tyler organized curriculum work around four questions, and purposes came first. Learning experiences, organization, and evaluation all follow from them. He did not leave purposes to any single authority. Planners draw on three sources and then filter the results through two screens.

Three sources, two screens

  • Studies of learners
  • Studies of contemporary life
  • Subject specialists
  • Screen: philosophy of education
  • Screen: psychology of learning

In practice

Before choosing activities for a unit, a team agrees on the purpose: students will be able to evaluate primary sources. Every later choice, from readings to the final assessment, is checked against that purpose.

Reference

Tyler, R. W. (1949). Basic principles of curriculum and instruction. University of Chicago Press.

Joseph Schwab · Balance

Who?

Whose perspectives must shape this decision?

Schwab argued that sound curriculum decisions give balanced attention to four commonplaces, and that no single one should dominate. Curriculum making is deliberation: people who represent each commonplace weigh alternatives together and reach defensible choices for particular students in a particular setting.

The four commonplaces

  • Learner
  • Teacher
  • Subject matter
  • Milieu

In practice

The teacher is one of the commonplaces, so teacher capacity and well-being belong in the balance. A mandate that expands what teachers must adapt, without attention to their time, preparation, and own needs, is out of balance by Schwab’s standard.

Reference

Schwab, J. J. (1973). The practical 3: Translation into curriculum. The School Review, 81(4), 501–522.

Carol Ann Tomlinson · Response

How?

What does this student need from me today?

Differentiation is a teacher’s responsive, intentional planning grounded in knowing students well. Teachers can adjust content, process, product, and learning environment in response to readiness, interest, and learning profile. Tomlinson describes readiness as a student’s current proximity to specific learning goals, so it moves as circumstances change.

What a teacher can adjust

  • Content
  • Process
  • Product
  • Learning environment

In practice

Differentiation does not wait for a diagnosis. After a family move or a loss, a student’s readiness may dip for a few weeks. The teacher adjusts the process and offers more choice, without requiring a label as the price of support.

Reflect

Reference

Tomlinson, C. A. (2014). The differentiated classroom: Responding to the needs of all learners (2nd ed.). ASCD.

The surrounding ring

The Milieu

What surrounds the work without being its core?

Policy, law, and community values shape every curriculum decision. The least restrictive environment (LRE) shows how. It began as a civil liberties principle, the least restrictive alternative, before it became an education requirement. LRE is a legal entitlement for students eligible under IDEA. Differentiation is a professional obligation to every student, with or without a label.

How LRE developed

  • Shelton v. Tucker (1960)
  • PARC v. Pennsylvania (1971–72)
  • Mills v. Board of Education (1972)
  • Section 504 (1973)
  • P.L. 94-142 (1975)
  • IDEA (1990)

In practice

Because LRE began as a legal principle rather than a pedagogical one, differentiation was later asked to carry legal weight it was never designed to bear. Recognizing that history helps explain much of the friction around implementation.
